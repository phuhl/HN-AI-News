    domain: cacm.acm.org
    summary: AI benchmarks are being gamed into uselessness — Goodhart's Law guarantees that every eval frontier models optimize
      toward stops measuring the capability it was designed to track.
    points: 75
    hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49126716
    comments: 32
    time: Aug 5, 19:37 UTC
    content_bullets:
    - Goodhart's Law states that when a measure becomes a target it ceases to be a good measure — in AI, this means training
      on benchmarks teaches models to pass tests, not develop the underlying skill.
    - 'Major evals like MMLU, HumanEval, MATH, and GSM8K have been effectively ''solved'': top models cluster near ceiling
      scores, stripping those benchmarks of any power to differentiate real capability.'
    - The benchmark lifecycle has compressed to months — as soon as a new eval gains traction, it becomes an optimization
      target and saturates before it can accumulate a useful track record.
    - 'Data contamination worsens the picture: benchmark test sets frequently appear in web-scale training corpora, meaning
      high scores may reflect memorization rather than generalization.'
    - Building robust replacements is structurally hard — quality benchmarks require scarce human domain expertise at scale,
      creating a lag that favors overfitting over genuine progress.
    discussion_bullets:
    - 'Commenters highlight a real business risk: companies selecting models by leaderboard scores may be systematically optimizing
      for benchmark-passing skill rather than actual task performance.'
    - The community points to a cost asymmetry — constructing rigorous new benchmarks demands significant expert time, while
      frontier models saturate existing ones in months, making the evaluation arms race unsustainable.
    - Data contamination is called out as a compounding factor that undermines even carefully designed evals once their test
      sets eventually surface in training data.
