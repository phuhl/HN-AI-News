  - title: A simple fix for LLM tail latency
    link: https://engineering.myhoai.com/posts/a-simple-fix-for-llm-tail-latency/
    domain: engineering.myhoai.com
    summary: 'A Simple Trick Cuts Large Language Model Worst-Case Response Times: Fire Every Request Twice'
    points: 42
    hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49295179
    comments: 17
    time: Aug 17, 20:59 UTC
    content_bullets:
    - The fix is 'hedged requests' — send each prompt to two inference endpoints simultaneously and return whichever response arrives first, immediately discarding the other.
    - Tail latency (the slowest ~1% of requests, or p99) in LLM inference is especially unpredictable due to variable queue depths, differing output token lengths, and provider-side spikes.
    - Worst-case cost is roughly 2x compute spend per request; in practice, the faster call often wins well before the slower one finishes, so the real cost premium is lower.
    - The approach is most valuable for user-facing, latency-sensitive products — interactive chat, autocomplete — where a long tail response feels broken to users.
    - 'Implementation is deliberately minimal: fire two async requests, await the first result, cancel the loser — no custom inference infrastructure required.'
    discussion_bullets:
    - Commenters who've deployed similar dual-connection strategies confirmed real p99 improvements, noting costs rarely hit the 2x ceiling because the faster request typically wins early in generation.
    - Simon Willison identified this as the established 'hedged requests' pattern from distributed systems; discussion focused on why LLM-specific variability (token-by-token generation, provider queuing) makes the gains larger here than in typical RPC settings.
    - Skeptics questioned the compute waste for large models and argued batch or background workloads need a different solution — priority-tiered queues rather than redundant firing.
