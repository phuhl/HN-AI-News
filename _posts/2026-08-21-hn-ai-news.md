    link: https://blog.curiousquail.com/im-upset-again-about-a-co-creator-of-rss-being-prosecuted-for-something-meta-is-doing-with-little-consequence/
    domain: blog.curiousquail.com
    summary: A blogger draws a pointed parallel between Aaron Swartz's federal prosecution for mass-downloading JSTOR papers and Meta's large-scale web scraping for AI model training — both technically public-data collection, but with sharply different legal outcomes
    points: 867
    hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49379550
    comments: 203
    time: Aug 20, 20:07 UTC
    content_bullets:
    - Aaron Swartz co-created RSS and was indicted in 2011 under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) for downloading ~4.8M JSTOR articles; he faced 35 years in prison and died by suicide in 2013.
    - Meta has scraped billions of public web pages and social media posts to train its Llama AI models; despite class-action copyright suits and EU investigations, no criminal charges have been filed.
    - The author argues the CFAA is selectively enforced — individuals liberating public-interest data face prosecution, while corporations extracting commercial AI training value do not.
    - The post invokes Aaron as "a co-creator of RSS" to sharpen the contrast between his open-web legacy and Meta's commercial data extraction.
    discussion_bullets:
    - Many commenters argued the legal distinction hinges on intent — Swartz gave content away while Meta claims fair-use exceptions — making the moral parallel stark even if legal theories differ.
    - A prominent thread held the real variable is corporate lobbying power, not the law's text, and that CFAA reform matters more than expecting consistent prosecution of large platforms.
    - Some noted that the EU's stricter approach to Meta's data practices may produce more meaningful accountability than US criminal law.

    - A dissenting thread argued that interaction model improvements are secondary to raw model quality, and that better models will make paradigm differences less important over time.
