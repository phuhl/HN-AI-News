    points: 21
    hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49316242
    comments: 10
    time: Aug 16, 02:20 UTC
    summary: 'Polite prompts, punished outputs: LLMs consistently give shorter and less sophisticated responses to female-coded
      phrasing like hedges and tag questions'
    content_bullets:
    - Prompts using hedges, tag questions, and collective reference — patterns associated with women's speech — consistently
      receive shorter, less sophisticated, less formal LLM responses across multiple document types.
    - 'Effect is driven by linguistic register, not explicit gender cues: sign-off names had no measurable impact, but phrasing
      style alone produced large, consistent disparities.'
    - Results held across four LLM models and three document types, suggesting this is a systemic pattern rather than a quirk
      of any single model.
    - Mechanistic analysis shows these linguistic features are encoded in early transformer layers and entangled with other
      features, making post-hoc mitigation strategies difficult.
    - Authors call for upstream training interventions to prevent the bias from encoding pre-existing social communication
      inequities into AI-mediated workplace productivity.
    discussion_bullets:
    - 'Commenters question ecological validity: the female-coded prompts were synthesized from a general corpus rather than
      actual prompts written by women, so real-world generalizability is uncertain.'
    - The findings may be model-size dependent — one commenter notes hedging can improve output quality on larger models by
      triggering more careful reasoning, yet the study tested only small or older models.
    - 'Several commenters argue the gender framing is too narrow: LLMs broadly calibrate response depth to inferred user expertise,
      reading hedging and politeness as lower confidence regardless of the author''s gender.'
